For the first activity, sending, receiving, and responding to email, I worked through the Gmail Basics tutorial to review everyday email communication and etiquette. I learned how to compose a clear message, use a subject line that tells the reader what the email is about, and understand the difference between "reply" and "reply all." That last one matters more than I expected, because reply-all can send a private note to an entire group by accident. I also learned to keep the tone professional and get to the point quickly. I can apply this right away at my job as a patient transport worker, where I email coworkers and supervisors, and in school when I message my professors and my advisor. Writing a clean, professional email makes me look more organized and usually gets me faster responses. The next activity was viewing email message headers and reading about email spoofing, which is really about how an email travels and how you can tell if a message is actually from who it claims to be. I learned that the "From" address can be faked, and that the header shows the true path the message took, including the sending servers and the authentication checks along the way. Before this, I never thought to look past the sender name. This turned out to be useful both for protecting myself and for running my online store. If I get a message that looks like it is from a payment processor or a supplier, I can check the header to confirm it is legitimate before clicking anything, which helps me avoid phishing and protects my customers at the same time. The last activity I completed was researching Internet mailing lists and bulk email restrictions, which covers how mailing lists work and the rules around sending email to a large group. I learned that legitimate bulk email has to be opt-in, needs a working unsubscribe link, and has to follow anti-spam laws like CAN-SPAM, or the messages end up blocked or marked as spam. This applies directly to my e-commerce store. If I want to send product updates or promotions to customers, I now understand that I have to build a real opt-in list and follow those rules so my emails actually reach people instead of landing in the spam folder. Together, these three activities gave me a much better handle on using email in a way that is professional, secure, and effective in both my current work and my plans.
