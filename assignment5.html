For the first activity, I completed the TutorialsPoint Social Networking tutorial. The topic was what social networking is and how different sites serve different purposes. I learned that social networking sites let users build a profile, connect with others, and share content, but each site focuses on something different. Some focus on personal connections, some on professional networking, and some on sharing photos or videos. I also learned that privacy settings matter because anything posted can be seen, saved, and shared by others. The sites that interested me most were LinkedIn and Instagram. I can apply this as a future healthcare worker by being careful about what I post publicly and never sharing anything related to patients or my workplace. For the second activity, I reviewed LearnFree's LinkedIn Basics and set up a LinkedIn profile. The topic was using LinkedIn as a professional networking tool. I learned that a LinkedIn profile works like an online resume, with a headline, summary, work experience, education, and skills. I also learned that connections, endorsements, and recommendations help build credibility, and that recruiters use LinkedIn to search for candidates. I can apply this by listing my hospital job and my Harper College coursework on my profile, connecting with classmates and instructors, and following local hospitals so I can find job openings when I finish the Radiologic Technology program. For the third activity, I reviewed the Honeycomb model from Smart Insights and used it to evaluate popular social media sites. The topic was the seven building blocks of social media: identity, conversations, sharing, presence, relationships, reputation, and groups. I learned that Facebook is the only site that supports the full honeycomb because it covers every element, including groups and online status. LinkedIn comes close and is strongest in identity, relationships, and reputation. YouTube and Instagram are strong in sharing and identity but weaker in groups and presence. Pinterest mostly focuses on sharing and lacks conversations and presence. Snapchat is strong in conversations and presence but lacks reputation and groups. Reddit is strong in groups, conversations, and reputation through karma, but weak in identity because users are mostly anonymous. Twitter supports conversations, sharing, and reputation but is weaker in groups, and Tumblr focuses on identity and sharing but lacks presence. I can apply this model by choosing the right platform for a specific goal. For example, if I want to promote my online store, I would focus on sites that are strong in sharing and reputation instead of trying to be active everywhere.
